How it was built

The problem in detail

Every month someone spends hours reading the dashboard and writing a short commentary for the owner: what went up, what went down, and what needs attention. It is useful, but it is slow, it depends on one person, and it is easy to miss something.

The goal: a summary that writes itself from the numbers, is the same every time for the same data, and can be checked line by line, without sending company data to a paid AI service.

How the logic works

  1. Compute the facts. For each month: net sales, gross margin, sales by category, branch and customer, receivables, DSO, overdue share and month-end cash.
  2. Run the rules. 8 rules each look for one kind of change (headline sales, seasonality, category drivers, margin, branch outliers, lost regular customers and big customer swings, receivables and DSO, cash). A rule only fires if the change passes a threshold, for example a margin move of at least 0.3 points or a category change above AED 15,000. On average 7.1 rules fire per month.
  3. Rank by importance. Every candidate sentence carries a materiality score in AED. The headline is always kept; then the four most material points are chosen.
  4. Fill the templates. Wording follows the numbers: within 1% is "broadly flat", above is "rose", below is "fell". A seasonal note is added when the same month last year moved the same way, so a normal Ramadan or summer swing is not reported as a problem.
  5. Split facts from actions. Explanations go in the main paragraph; customer, receivables and cash points go into a watch list.

Because it is rules and templates, the same data always gives the same text, and every sentence can be traced back to a number. Months summarised: 12 (October 2025 to September 2026; the first year of data provides the comparisons). Every summary is generated by summarise.py; nothing was edited by hand.

Adding an LLM, only with client approval. A language model can make the wording more natural. If a client wants this, only the computed figures (no customer names, or pseudonymised names) would be sent to a model running under the client's own agreement, such as Azure OpenAI in their Microsoft tenant. The rules stay the source of truth, a check confirms that every number in the AI text matches the inputs, and the rules-only version remains the fallback. No data goes to an AI service without written approval.

How it would work in your business

Tools

  • Python (open source) or Power BI measures for the facts and rules
  • Power Automate to run after the monthly refresh and send the summary by email or Teams
  • Optional: Power BI smart narrative visual, or Copilot where the client's licences include it
  • Optional, with written approval: an LLM in the client's own Azure tenant to polish wording

What you provide

  • An agreed list of KPIs and thresholds that matter to management
  • Access to the reporting model or the same exports used for the dashboard
  • Feedback on two or three draft summaries to tune the wording
  • A decision on whether an LLM may be used, and under which agreement

Indicative effort: about 1 to 2 weeks once the reporting model exists, longer if the KPIs still need defining. The scope and a fixed price are confirmed after the paid reporting diagnostic. Nothing on this page is a client result.
