Distributor sales and stock automation

Daily sales, stock ageing and reorder alerts for a three-depot distributor, with a weekly email that builds itself. See exactly how it works, step by step.

The problem

A distributor with three depots sells around 290 items to about 450 shops, restaurants and hotels. Sales come out of the accounting system, stock is checked in spreadsheets, and the buyer reorders once a week from memory and a printout. By the time someone notices that a fast seller is short, it is already out of stock in one depot, while slow items sit on the shelf for months.

Management wants three things: daily sales they can trust, an early warning when items need ordering, and a clear view of old stock. And they want a short summary in their inbox every Monday without anyone building it by hand.

Approach

  1. Land the exports. Daily exports (sales lines, stock on hand, open purchase orders) are saved to a SharePoint folder, either scheduled from the accounting system or by a Power Automate flow.
  2. Clean and model. Power Query appends and cleans the files; the model is a simple star schema with sales, stock and purchase facts and item, customer, branch and date dimensions.
  3. Define the rules once. Days of cover = stock on hand divided by average daily demand over the last 90 days. An item needs ordering when stock on hand plus stock on order is below its reorder level. Stock-out risk = days of cover shorter than the supplier lead time. Stock age comes from receipt-date lots, first in, first out.
  4. Refresh and alert. The dataset refreshes every morning; the reorder list and the weekly email are generated from the same model, so the numbers always match the dashboard.

Here the same rules are written in Python (analyse_sales_stock.py) so that anyone can rerun them; in a client build they become Power BI measures.

How Power Automate would send it

  1. Recurrence trigger: every Monday at 7:00 am, UAE time.
  2. Run a query against a dataset (Power BI connector): pulls last week's KPIs, branch totals, top customers and the reorder list from the published model, so the email always matches the dashboard.
  3. Create HTML table and Compose: places the values into an HTML template like the one above.
  4. Send an email (V2) (Office 365 Outlook) to the management list, and optionally Post message in a chat or channel in Microsoft Teams.
  5. Run after failure branch: if the refresh failed or a query returns nothing, a short alert goes to the report owner instead of a wrong email.

How it would work for a real client

Tools

  • Power BI (Pro licences) for the dashboard and scheduled refresh
  • Power Query to clean and combine exports
  • SharePoint or OneDrive as the landing folder for exports
  • Power Automate for the weekly email, Teams posts and failure alerts
  • Optional: Python for one-off data checks during the diagnostic

What you provide

  • Regular exports of sales invoices, item master and stock on hand (by batch or receipt date if available)
  • Open purchase orders and any existing reorder levels
  • Microsoft 365 with Power BI Pro licences
  • A contact who can answer questions for 1 to 2 hours a week
  • The list of people who should receive the weekly email

Indicative effort: about 2 to 4 weeks of build after the diagnostic, depending on the number of data sources and branches. The scope and a fixed price are confirmed after the paid reporting diagnostic. Nothing on this page is a client result.
